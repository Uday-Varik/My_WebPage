Forecasting / FedEx, Memphis, Tennessee / Production system

Delivery delay, scored live.

Problem. Dispatchers needed a signal while a package was still moving, not a report the next morning.

Decision and tradeoff. I chose live scoring over batch reporting. Batch reporting was simpler to build, but it arrived too late to change a route. Live scoring cost more engineering, and it was the only option that could change a decision the same day.

Demand data→XGBoost, LightGBM, Prophet→SageMaker forecast→Dispatch planning Live events→Lambda and SageMaker→Delay score→Dispatch reroute

What I built. Demand forecasting on SageMaker, and a real-time delay scoring service on Lambda and SageMaker that answers in under 60 milliseconds, more than 50,000 times a day.

Result. Forecast accuracy up 31% over the prior baseline.

Links. Live demo: not available. Code: not public. This was an internal production system.

Language / FedEx, Memphis, Tennessee / Production system

Complaints, routed by reading them.

Problem. Routing a complaint depended on who picked up the ticket, so the same complaint could take different paths.

Decision and tradeoff. I chose a trained classifier with retrieval over keyword rules. Rules were easier to explain, but they broke on new wording. A trained model handled new wording, and retrieval grounded its answer in internal knowledge instead of guessing.

Complaint text→BERT classifier→LangChain RAG, internal knowledge→Right team, with an answer

What I built. A fine-tuned BERT classifier paired with a LangChain RAG pipeline over internal knowledge bases, so a complaint is classified, matched to the right internal answer, and sent to the right team.

Result. Escalation handling time reduced.

Links. Live demo: not available. Code: not public. This was an internal production system.

Forecasting / Novartis, India / Production system

No shows, predicted with context.

Problem. Clinics lost whole days to empty rooms, and scheduling ignored why people missed appointments.

Decision and tradeoff. I chose a model with explanations over a more complex model without them. The complex model might have scored slightly higher, but clinic staff would not have trusted it. Explanations were the difference between a score and a scheduling change.

Appointment, behaviour, place, time, weather→XGBoost, logistic regression→Scoring API→Clinic scheduling SHAP explanation→Clinic team

What I built. A no-show prediction model using behavioural, demographic, and geospatial data with temporal and weather features, served through a real-time scoring API with SHAP explanations, more than 10,000 predictions a day.

Result. About 20% performance improvement over the prior approach.

Links. Live demo: not available. Code: not public. This was an internal production system.

Risk / Caliber Home Loans, India / Production system

Lending risk, in small patterns.

Problem. A loan that goes bad shows up months later, in small patterns, and data from three sources did not agree.

Decision and tradeoff. I chose to fix the data first, before tuning the model. Tuning first would have looked faster, but a model trained on disagreeing data would have learned the disagreement. Consolidation and quality checks came first, and the model came second.

Origination, servicing, underwriting→Snowflake→XGBoost, survival analysis→Risk score Great Expectations checks→Power BI KPIs

What I built. Prepayment and delinquency models, Snowflake ETL and ELT, automated amortization and loan schedules in Python, and data-quality checks, with Power BI dashboards for senior stakeholders.

Result. About $50,000 a year saved by fixing queries and warehouse settings.

Links. Live demo: not available. Code: not public. This was an internal production system.

About

to ship.

I started in healthcare, where a model had to earn the trust of the people who used it every day. Then I worked in lending, where the cost of a wrong answer arrived months later. Process

How I work.

  1. 01

    Understand the problem

    Who acts on the output, and what happens if it is wrong? That question comes before any model.

  2. 02

    Design and build

    Build it where it will live, with the checks it will face in production: versioning, drift, and explanation.

  3. 03

    Measure and improve

    Watch what happens after launch, test the change, and keep what works. A model earns its keep after it ships.

When the simpler model can be explained, checked, and fixed by the team that will live with it. Complexity has to buy a decision, not just a score.

When the work repeats, the checks are clear, and a person still makes the final call. Automation without a guardrail is not a shortcut.

Fix the data first. A model trained on disagreeing data learns the disagreement.
